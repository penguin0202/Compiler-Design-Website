So what's all this gotta do with programming languages? Well, we as humans need to find a way to communicate to computers our intentions, computers who only "speak" ones and zeroes. We don't speak ones and zeroes (frankly, we don't want to), in fact, we speak "natural languages" that are far too messy and ambiguous for computers to understand. So we need find some sort of mediator, a intermediate language, or a bridge, if you will (see? A metaphor), that can connect human languages to machine language. Hmm...I wonder what that is...OH YEAH! It's a programming language! A programming language's purpose is for it to be easily understandable to humans (if you take the time to learn, that is), all the while maintaining a rigidity that can allow for it to be easily translated into ones and zeroes that computers can understand. (if you're wondering why programming languages seem distant from our natural langauges, this is the simplification of the why)
