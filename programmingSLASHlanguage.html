The languages of humans on my other limb are much less rigid than ones and zeroes. Take the English langauge for example (get ready for a rapid-fire): New words are created with time. Definitions of words evolve gradually. Words with synonymous definitions have connotations that differently affect meaning. Meaning that can be emphasized though capitalizing or quoting. Meaning also sometimes derived from speaker tone. Or from the social environment. Or from the current cultural phenomenon. Or from the audience's emotions and feelings with certain words. Sometimes quoting words can be sarcastic, casting another shadow of doubt on whether things mean what they mean. And on doubting meaning, metaphors aren't to be taken literally. Most times, even with context, ambiguity persists. Perhaps the most used example is whether "she" in "Mary beat her daughter because she was drunk" referred to Mary or her daughter. Really, there just isn't a correct-ness to all of it. Language is fluid; with even its punctuations. Why do humans speak like this? I don't know I'm not a language expert. But what I can repeat is that human languages (dubbed "natural languages") are ambiguous, feeling-driven, and complicated.

So what's all this gotta do with programming languages? Well, they are a sort of intermediate languages for us to communicate to computers our intentions.
